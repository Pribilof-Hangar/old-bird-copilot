OLD BIRD COPILOT  -  version 1.5.0
Spoken landing callouts for Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024,
made for classic aircraft that don't have any (like the PMDG DC-6).
Works with any aircraft.

MADE TO FLY WITH THE DC-6's FLIGHT ENGINEER (AFE)
The AFE runs the engines, flaps, gear and checklists. Old Bird Copilot is the
rest of the crew: the first officer calling speeds, heights and warnings,
checking you're stable and grading your landing. It works without the AFE
too, and in other aircraft.


WHAT YOU NEED
- Windows 11
- Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024 (tested with the Microsoft Store version;
  the Steam version uses the same connection but hasn't been tested yet)
- Nothing else. No SPAD.next, FSUIPC, Axis and Ohs, MobiFlight or Python.
  Everything it needs is included, voice too. It works with or without the
  PMDG DC-6, and nothing is added to the simulator or its Community folder.


INSTALLING
1. Double-click OldBirdCopilot-Setup-<version>.exe.
2. Windows may show "Windows protected your PC". That's because this free
   app isn't signed with a paid certificate. If you downloaded it from the
   official Old Bird Copilot page, click "More info", then "Run anyway".
3. Follow the setup. It installs for your Windows user only, so it doesn't
   ask for administrator rights. Tick "Create a desktop shortcut" if you
   want one.
4. Start it from the Start menu: "Old Bird Copilot".

Prefer no installer? The portable zip works too: right-click it, choose
"Extract All..." into e.g. Documents, and run OldBirdCopilot.exe from there.
The portable copy keeps its settings in its own folder.


USING IT
- Start it whenever you like, before or after the sim. It connects by itself.
  The dot at the top turns green: "Connected to MSFS".
- Before take-off, fill in "Takeoff" (optional): V1, VR, V2. You'll hear
  "vee one", "rotate" and "positive rate". In the DC-6, the "Aircraft" switch at
  the top moves from UNIVERSAL to DC-6 by itself, and the app suggests these
  from your live weight - click Use. Click either side to choose yourself.
- Before each approach, fill in "Landing" (all optional):
    Vref         your landing speed. Enables "plus ten" / "minus five" calls on final,
                 and "ref" once you're back on speed.
    Minimums     in feet above the runway (the number in brackets on the chart).
    Max landing  a touchdown limit in fpm, e.g. 200 for a soft-landing mission.
                 Leave it empty if there isn't one.
- On approach you'll hear the heights (2500, 1000, 500, 100, 50, 40, 30,
  20, 10), speed deviations, flap settings and warnings like "sink rate".
- Passing 1000 ft (or as you start down on final, on a low circuit): the wind
  ("wind two seven zero at fifteen, crosswind ten from the left"). Passing 500 ft: "five hundred, stable" - or "unstable" and
  what's off (speed, sink rate, gear, flaps). In a strong crosswind (25 kt+)
  your copilot adds a remark like "these are some crosswinds!"
- Type your destination's airport code (e.g. PASN) in "Destination". The
  Descent panel then shows the distance, when to start down ("in 12 nm"),
  where you should be right now ("7,400 ft"), how far off you are ("2,200
  high") and the descent rate that still gets you there. Your copilot says
  "request descent" 2 minutes before the top of descent (time to ask ATC),
  "top of descent" at the right point, and "high on profile" if you're well
  above the path.
  The plan in DC-6 mode: 1,000 fpm at cruise power down to a level-off 3,000 ft
  above the field about 30 nm out, then slow down level for the approach.
  You'll hear "five hundred to level off" on the way down.
  Other planes: 1,000 fpm, to be 1,500 ft above the field 5 nm out (change it
  in Settings > Landing).
- "Calculator..." (under Destination) answers "I'm at 15,000 ft, when do I
  start down?". It uses your live altitude and groundspeed (or numbers you
  type), and a slider for the descent rate (1,000 fpm to start with; slide it
  for a gentler or faster descent). The slider also sets when "top of descent"
  is said.
- Flying something that already calls out "100, 50, 40..." by itself (most
  modern airliners)? Untick "Height callouts" in the main window. Everything
  else keeps working. Tick it again for the old birds.
- Under the readouts you can see your live weight and fuel - handy for finding
  the right Vref for your load (e.g. on a NeoFly freight job).
- In the PMDG DC-6 it looks the Vref up for you: "Suggested Vref" appears
  in the Landing panel (from PMDG's own landing speed table) - click Use.
  With DC-6 mode on, speed calls start once landing flaps (40 or full) are set.
- DC-6 landing check: passing 300 ft on final, your copilot says "flaps full,
  three green" - or "check flaps" / "check gear" if something isn't set yet.
- After you land, the Landings tab shows your touchdown rate.
- Test voice and Demo approach let you hear it without flying.
- Callouts ON/OFF mutes it. Settings... changes voice, heights and more.
- The voice is "Michael", a natural-sounding voice built into the app (no
  internet needed). Prefer a Windows voice? Settings... > Voice > Windows default.


YOUR SETTINGS AND RECORDINGS
They're kept in your Windows user folder. To find them: About > "Open that
folder" (or type %APPDATA%\Old Bird Copilot in File Explorer's address bar).
  config.toml   your settings
  flights       recordings of your recent flights (the newest 20 are kept)
  sounds        optional custom .wav callouts
(The portable zip keeps these next to OldBirdCopilot.exe instead.)


UPDATING
Run the new OldBirdCopilot-Setup. It replaces the old version and keeps
your settings and recordings. If the app is open, setup asks you to close it.


UNINSTALLING
Windows Settings > Apps > Installed apps > Old Bird Copilot > Uninstall.
It asks whether to also remove your settings and flight recordings - the
answer is "No" unless you choose otherwise, so a reinstall picks them up again.


IF SOMETHING'S WRONG
- Stuck on "Waiting for MSFS": load into a flight. The sim has to be past the
  main loading screen.
- No sound: click "Test voice" and check your Windows sound output.
- Robotic voice instead of Michael: look in the Activity tab - it says why.
  Running setup again usually fixes it. For the portable copy, moving its
  folder to e.g. C:\Old Bird Copilot fixes it.
- Your antivirus quarantines OldBirdCopilot.exe: small homemade apps
  sometimes get flagged by mistake. Restore it and mark the folder as safe.
- Portable zip: settings don't save? Don't run it from inside the zip or
  from Program Files - extract it to Documents first.
- It says it "hit an error and closed": send the file
  landing-callouts-error.log from your settings folder (see above).
- Anything else odd during a flight: press "Flag moment" right then, and
  tell me roughly when it happened.
